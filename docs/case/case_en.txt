SUMMARY
The spirit of the challenge
The challenge favors a self-explaining agentic solution. Three
investor profiles provide reference scenarios. Teams are free to
choose their assets, use case, and architecture. The goal is to
produce useful results and explain their sources, assumptions,
and limitations.
--- PAGE 3 ---
1. CHALLENGE
Context
Investors have access to a wealth of information: market prices, company
disclosures, macroeconomic conditions, financial news, and risk
indicators. The challenge is not only to access this information, but also to
select it, connect it, and draw understandable conclusions for
different personal circumstances. 
Artificial intelligence creates opportunities to rethink analysis and
decision-making. Its usefulness depends, however, on data quality, the
methods used, and the ability to acknowledge uncertainty
and limitations.
Description
Your challenge: design a self-explaining agentic solution that uses
public financial data to inform investment analysis or decisions for
different investor profiles. The solution should explain its results; teams
may choose their assets, tools, and architecture.
The intended direction is agentic: one or more agents may use data and
tools, coordinate tasks, and adapt their analysis to the context. A solution
might compare assets, explore scenarios, explain risks, or present a
hypothetical allocation. These examples leave teams free to choose
their use case, methods, and solution format.
The goal is not to select the portfolio with the highest historical return.
Teams are encouraged to demonstrate a credible, innovative, and
useful approach suited to the problem they choose to solve.

1. CHALLENGE
SUGGESTED APPROACH
The following are possible steps, with no prescribed order or architecture:
Frame a useful question: identify the financial need or decision the
solution is intended to inform.
Design an agentic approach: describe the roles of the agent or
agents, the data and tools they use, their coordination, and their
controls.
Consider the context: account for the personas and, where
relevant, other explicit objectives, preferences, or constraints.
Test the results: examine sensitivity to different assumptions, data,
or scenarios where appropriate.
Make the solution self-explaining: connect results to sources,
assumptions, observable steps, and limitations so users can
understand and verify what supports a conclusion.
GUIDANCE
A focused, verifiable agentic solution is encouraged. Technical
sophistication and the number of agents are not ends in themselves.
Teams may present a simulated portfolio, a comparative analysis, a
decision-support tool, or another defensible outcome. Comparing two
contrasting investor situations can show adaptability without requiring a
uniform output.

2. DATA
STARTER DATASET STRUCTURE
The starter dataset provides a common baseline. It is optional: teams
may supplement it or use other public data suited to their solution:
assets_universe.csv: 31 assets: 10 Canadian equities, 10 U.S.
equities, 9 ETFs covering equities, bonds, gold, and one ESG-screened
fund, plus Bitcoin and Ether. Fields: identifier, ticker, name, asset class,
market, currency, and sector.
prices_daily.csv: about 40,000 rows of daily prices: date, ticker,
open, high, low, close, adjusted close (dividends and splits), volume,
and listing currency.
corporate_actions.csv, fx_usdcad_daily.csv, and
macro_rates.csv: dividends and splits by ex-date; daily USD/CAD
exchange rate (Bank of Canada); 9 macro series: policy rates, 2- and
10-year yields, and CPI for Canada and the U.S., plus U.S.
unemployment (Bank of Canada and FRED).
sources_metadata.csv, build_dataset.py, and README.md:
source, date, processing, limitations, and terms for each file, and the
script that rebuilds the data. No ESG scores, news, or company
fundamentals are included.

To go further
Teams may add, for example, ESG data, news, company
fundamentals or other assets if this information serves their
approach. For each source used, they explain its relevance,
origin, date, processing and limitations, as well as the
applicable access and reuse conditions.
PUBLIC SOURCES AND USEFUL RESOURCES
According to needs and applicable access conditions, several sources
may be consulted:
Company publications: annual reports, continuous disclosure
documents, earnings releases, investor relations pages.
Regulatory filings: SEC EDGAR and SEDAR+, for relevant accessible
filings.
Macroeconomic and rate data: Bank of Canada, FRED and
Statistics Canada.
Fund data: ETF issuer websites, product factsheets, holdings lists
and published documents.
Prices and market series: freely accessible data providers, subject
to applicable licences and API limits.

TIME PERIOD, QUALITY, AND RULES OF USE
The starter dataset covers five years of data from November 12, 2021
to November 12, 2026, which includes 2022, when stocks and bonds fell
together. Teams that add other data should specify the dates and
frequencies they use.
Traceability: identify the source, date, currency, and relevant
processing.
Time consistency: distinguish observation dates from publication
dates; do not use information in a simulation before it was available.
Comparability: consider dividends, splits, selection bias, missing
data, and CAD/USD conversions where relevant.
Licences: respect access, retention, and redistribution terms.
Privacy: do not use real client accounts or confidential banking
information. Portfolios and profiles may be entirely fictional. 
Optional scenarios include a cautious investor preparing
for a near-term purchase, a balanced investor pursuing
multiple objectives, or an aggressive investor seeking long-
term growth. Teams may define their own fictional
scenarios.

3. EXPECTED DELIVERABLES
The jury should be able to understand the value proposition, observe the
solution, and understand its agentic approach and the evidence
supporting its results. The formats below are inspired by the 2025
Datathon.
1. Prototype and source code
A demonstrable prototype showing a complete use case and an
accessible explanation of the result, including sources, assumptions,
key factors, and limitations.
A code repository and README summarizing dependencies, data
sources, configuration, and setup or reproduction instructions.
2. Demonstration video
A short video, approximately five minutes as a guide, showing an end-to-
end example: the chosen problem, the role of the agent or agents, data
and tools used, the result, and the explanation presented to the user. Two
distinct investor situations may illustrate adaptation to context, without
prescribing an output format.
3. Technical documentation
Provide a concise document describing:
The overall logic, roles and interactions of the agent or agents, and
any controls or governance checks used.
The methods, models, or technologies used and why they were chosen.
Sources, selected assets, data processing, and assumptions.
The explainability mechanism and an example connecting a
conclusion to data or observable execution traces.
Validation of results and explanations, limitations, and proposed
improvements.
Teams may choose their tools, infrastructure, models, and services unless
the official technical rules specify otherwise.

Key takeaway
The jury should understand what the solution does and what
supports its results. An agentic, self-explaining approach is
sought. The personas and example assets leave teams free to
choose the use case, architecture, fictional allocation, and
form of output.

--- PAGE 17 ---
The assessment will recognize the overall value of each project rather
than comparing only simulated returns, standard portfolios, or the
number of models used.
The jury will consider the relevance of the problem, strength of the
approach, creativity, execution quality, and usefulness to users. It will
value the relevance of the agentic approach and the solution’s ability to
explain its own results using data, assumptions, and limitations.
Teams remain free to choose the architecture, number of agents, models,
tools, and result presentation. An explainability agent is one possibility. A
convincing demonstration and explanations faithful to data and
observable actions matter more than complexity or historical asset
performance.
Responsible boundaries
The challenge takes place entirely in simulation. Prototypes must not
execute real transactions or present themselves as providing
personalized financial advice. Teams must distinguish facts, assumptions,
and fictional scenarios, and communicate material uncertainties clearly.
Explanations should be concise, verifiable, and faithful to sources and
observable actions, without inventing justifications or exposing detailed
internal model reasoning.

Historical performance and risk
Historical data help describe assets and inform their suitability for the
personas, for example through returns and volatility calculated over an
explicit period. Volatility is one indicator among others: investment
horizon, diversification, and investor preferences also matter. 
The challenge does not ask teams to predict future returns from
past performance. Past performance does not guarantee future
results. Teams should explain their indicators, limitations, and
uncertainties rather than automatically matching a profile to an asset.
